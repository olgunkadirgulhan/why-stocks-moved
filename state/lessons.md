## Audit (STOP / KEEP / TEST)
### STOP
* **Generic Dollar/Macro Updates:** Underperform heavily compared to asset-specific posts (e.g., "Dollar up 0.86% as commodities slide" at **0.36x**).
* **Vague Apple & Tech Market Summaries:** Broader context updates lack pull (e.g., "Apple drops 1.63% while tech surges" at **0.14x**).
* **Broad Index Divergence Posts:** Generic index comparison updates consistently flop (e.g., "Nasdaq up 22% while Dow drops" at **0.18x**).
* **"The Statistic" Hook Formula:** Performed worse than "The Question" format across test cases (e.g., "Tesla 7.89% rally hits a wall" at **0.32x** and "Nasdaq up 1.19% while Dow lags behind" at **0.93x**).
* **Comparisons with Zero Context/Call-to-Action:** Broader comparison attempts failed completely to get engagement (e.g., "Amazon 3.29% rally leaves Nvidia behind" at **0.0x**).

---

### KEEP
* **Gold Asset Updates (SAVE Goal):** Your strongest overall performer by a wide margin (e.g., "Gold fell 2.38% to $4,195.90" at **4.57x** and "Gold $4,219.80 gain beats USD drop" at **4.54x**).
* **Meta Stock Movement Updates:** Highly engaging individual stock target (e.g., "Meta stock fell 2.38% now" at **3.00x** and "Meta stock rose 3.24% on AI meeting" at **1.54x**).
* **Oil Sector Impact Posts:** Actionable commodity updates that highlight sector ripple effects perform strongly (e.g., "WTI oil down 1.64% hits 3 sectors" at **2.93x** vs general oil at **0.96x**).
* **Single Stock Market Impact (Nvidia/S&P):** High-profile stock catalysts tied to broader impact generate strong reach (e.g., "Nvidia up 2.12% to $238.90 hits indexes" at **2.29x**).

---

### TEST
* **Multi-Asset Listicles vs. Single Updates:** Test structured list formats for SAVE content (e.g., "3 Commodities Crashing This Week" achieved **1.11x** vs single-commodity updates like Dollar at **0.36x**).
* **Standardized Tesla Price Action Format:** Tesla performance varies wildly across identical titles (ranges from **0.32x** to **2.14x**); test fixed visual formats/hooks to stabilize performance.
* **Converting FOLLOW Goals to SAVE Goals on Tech Winners:** Individual tech posts succeed on views but fail on follow goals; test adding save-worthy resource hooks to high-view tech posts (e.g., converting "MSFT +3.39% beats NVDA -2.00%" at **1.50x** to a SAVE objective).

## Trends (TAKE / ADAPT / SIT OUT)
You are an analyst looking at the last 7 days of finance data. We have heavy outliers from legacy media (WSJ, CNBC) and personal brands (Graham Stephan, the bald trader). 

Here is what is actually moving the needle:

### 1. FORMATS MOVING NOW
*   **The "Invisible Giant" Case Study:** Taking a household name and revealing a massive, non-obvious financial figure.
    *   *Examples:* WSJ’s "Twinkies... $5 Billion Problem" (113x median) and CNBC’s "How Costco keeps gas so cheap" (34x median).
    *   *Why it spreads:* It uses a "Pattern Interrupt." You think you know Costco/Twinkies, but you don't know the *number* behind them.
*   **The Aggressive Tier List:** Fast-paced ranking of financial products.
    *   *Examples:* Graham Stephan’s "Ranking the BEST Credit Cards" (42x median) and "BEST Money Books" (10x median).
    *   *Why it spreads:* High information density. It promises to do the homework for the viewer in under 60 seconds.
*   **The "60-Second Mastery":** Ultra-fast educational breakdowns of complex charts or systems.
    *   *Examples:* the bald trader’s "Candlestick Patterns Explained in 60 Seconds" (13x median).
    *   *Why it spreads:* It lowers the barrier to entry for "hard" finance topics.

### 2. THE ANGLE UNDERNEATH
*   **Case Studies:** **Curiosity/Relief.** "I always wondered why they did that, now I feel smarter."
*   **Tier Lists:** **Status/Security.** "Do I have the 'best' card? Am I reading the 'best' books?"
*   **AI Stock/Bubble Watch:** **Fear of Missing Out (FOMO).** (MarketBeat/Forbes). The viewer is looking for a shortcut to the next NVIDIA.

### 3. FIT FOR ME
*   **The "Invisible Giant" Case Study: `TAKE IT`**
    *   *Reason:* This is the highest leverage format. It builds authority and gets massive reach by piggybacking on famous brands.
*   **The Aggressive Tier List: `TAKE IT`**
    *   *Reason:* These are "Save" magnets. People save these videos to reference later, which tells the algorithm your content is high-value.
*   **The AI Hype/Bubble News: `ADAPT IT`**
    *   *Reason:* Unless you are a news desk, reporting on "Anthropic pauses perks" is too dry. Only do this if you can link it to a specific action the viewer should take with their own money.
*   **Geopolitical Scares (Plague/Security): `SIT IT OUT`**
    *   *Reason:* CNBC and Forbes (17x–29x) are winning here because they are news outlets. For a finance creator, this feels like "doom-bait" and doesn't build a loyal, action-oriented audience.

### 4. FIRST MOVE (For "TAKE IT" items)
*   **For the Case Study:** 
    *   *Hook:* "Everyone thinks [Brand] makes money from [Product]... they're wrong. They actually make [Large $ Amount] from [Secret Strategy]."
    *   *Opening Shot:* You standing in front of the physical store or holding the product, then a fast cut to a massive red "$-Billion" graphic.
*   **For the Tier List:**
    *   *Hook:* "I spent 40 hours comparing every [Product/Card/App] so you don't have to. Here is the only Tier List you need for 2024."
    *   *Opening Shot:* A literal "S-Tier to F-Tier" chart on screen with logos flying into the slots immediately.

### 5. FADING: The "Simple Comparison"
While *the bald trader* got a 10x multiple on "SIP vs FD," simple 1v1 comparisons are being outperformed by "Tier Lists" (40x+). If you're going to compare things, compare **all** of them, not just two. The audience currently wants the "Definitive Guide," not just a "This or That."

**Ranked Priority:**
1. **The Invisible Giant Case Study** (Highest reach potential)
2. **Aggressive Tier List** (Highest save/retention potential)
3. **60-Second Mastery** (Highest "expert" positioning)
